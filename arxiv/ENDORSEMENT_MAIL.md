The paper is titled "A Pythagoras Tree Grown from a Dodecahedron" (11
pages, 7 figures).  In short:

- Bosman's Pythagoras tree (1942) is built from squares, and the known
  three-dimensional versions from cubes; no dodecahedral version seems to
  exist, and the classical construction cannot be copied literally because
  regular pentagons have no right angles.
- We document a tree built from regular dodecahedra — each dodecahedron
  carrying five scaled, twisted children on the edges of one pentagonal
  face — modelled as an iterated function system with condensation, with a
  short computer-assisted proof that it never self-collides, and a
  Pythagorean identity that singles out the scale 1/√5 and the twist
  arccos(1/√5), giving the crown a Hausdorff dimension of exactly 2.
- A parity argument shows the tree cannot grow on all twelve faces at
  once, but smaller scale factors admit certified clash-free all-faces
  trees.
